Home care services in Israel

Families often reach out to us during a difficult time. A parent may have just returned home from hospital, or everyday tasks they once managed independently may have become more challenging. Our first step is to understand the situation and assess what level of support is truly needed.

Caregivers provide assistance with personal care such as washing and dressing, mobility support, meal preparation, medication reminders, and light household tasks. But care is also about connection. Having someone to talk to, and someone who helps your parent stay active and engaged, can make a meaningful difference in daily wellbeing.

Care can range from a few hours a week to a live-in arrangement, and it evolves as needs change. We regularly review the care plan to ensure it continues to match each family's situation. More information is available on our home care and full services pages.

Elderly care, senior care and nursing care at home

Most older people would prefer to remain in their own home. Familiar surroundings and routines can play an important part in helping them to function well. The practical question is what level of support is required to make that possible.

We begin with an in-home assessment. A care coordinator meets with the family and with the person who will be receiving care, taking the time to understand both perspectives. We then look for a caregiver who suits the household, rather than simply the first person available. Factors such as language, temperament, smoking, pets and religious observance can all influence whether a placement is successful, so we prefer to discuss these matters at the start to ensure a good match.

Our caregivers are professionally trained and receive ongoing support from our team throughout their employment, not just during the first few weeks. You can read more about the caregivers we place with families and the types of nursing and personal care we provide.

Round-the-clock and live-in care

Hourly support is no longer sufficient once a person cannot safely be left alone. Live-in care means a caregiver lives in the home and is present overnight. It does not mean that one person is on duty 24 hours a day; rest periods and time off must be built into the arrangement from the beginning. In practice, families usually move to live-in care following a fall, or when dementia progresses to the point where wandering becomes a risk.

An alternative is shift-based care, where several caregivers rotate so that the person on duty is always well rested. This arrangement suits households that do not have room to accommodate a live-in caregiver. Further details are available on our live-in caregiver page.

Employing a foreign caregiver

In Israel, live-in care usually involves employing a foreign caregiver, and this process begins with obtaining a permit. Permits are issued by the Population and Immigration Authority.

To be eligible, the person requiring care must be an Israeli resident above retirement age, living at home or in sheltered housing rather than in an institution, and in need of supervision and assistance with daily activities according to the National Insurance dependency criteria. You submit an application with the supporting documents to a Population and Immigration Authority office and pay the required fee. In urgent cases, such as when a person is being discharged from hospital, a temporary three-month permit can be issued.

It is worth clarifying one point, as it often surprises families: with this arrangement, you are the employer, not us. The permit is issued in the name of the person receiving care, and the household assumes the employer's responsibilities for wages, working conditions and severance pay. This differs from hourly care arranged through the benefit, where the caregiver is employed through us. We are happy to explain which arrangement applies to your situation before you make any commitment.

Once the permit has been approved, you register with a licensed private employment bureau from the list published on the Authority's website, and that bureau manages the placement. We would be glad to handle this on your behalf. We look for a caregiver who suits the household rather than the first person available, and we help prepare the family for the practical aspects of sharing a home with a caregiver. If a placement does not work out, please speak with us about the available options.

Our step-by-step guides explain how to obtain a permit and what employing a foreign caregiver involves.

The long-term care benefit from Bituach Leumi

The long-term care benefit (gimlat si'ud) is paid by the National Insurance Institute, known in Israel as Bituach Leumi, and for most families it helps cover a significant portion of the cost. It is usually provided as weekly care hours rather than as a payment. At the highest level, it is possible to receive a monthly cash benefit instead; we recommend confirming with Bituach Leumi which option applies in your case.

Eligibility is determined by the state rather than by us. Once you file a claim, an assessor appointed by Bituach Leumi, usually a nurse, physiotherapist or occupational therapist, carries out a dependency test covering washing, dressing, eating, mobility and cognitive function. This is generally conducted by telephone with the individual or a family member, although it may also take place as a visit to the home. The results determine which of the six levels applies.

The number of hours depends on the level and on who provides the care.

  • With an Israeli caregiver, the levels provide 5.5, 10, 15, 21, 26 and 30 hours per week.
  • If you employ a foreign caregiver, the allocation is lower from level three upward: 12, 17, 22 and 26 hours.

This is worth considering before choosing an option, as the difference at the highest level is four hours per week.

Claims take time to process, and families often need support in the meantime. While a claim is pending, we provide interim care free of charge for up to 60 days, from a minimum of six hours per week, so that families are not left managing on their own during the assessment period. We also assist with preparing the claim, and our eligibility calculator offers an initial indication of what you may be entitled to before you apply. Consultation and guidance are provided at no cost.

You can find more information in our benefit guide, our eligibility calculator, and our guides to the claim process, preparing for the dependency test, and the claim forms.

Alzheimer's and dementia care

Dementia care requires something different from a caregiver, and not everyone is suited to this type of work. Consistency is what helps most: the same person, the same routine, and the same order of activities each morning. Agitation tends to increase when the environment keeps changing, which is why a stable placement is especially important.

We look for caregivers experienced in supporting people with cognitive decline, who can manage the aspects families find most difficult, including handling repeated questions, restlessness at night, and the challenge of no longer being recognised by the person they care for. You can find more information on our Alzheimer's care and dementia information pages.

Care for IDF disabled veterans

Through our subsidiary Nativ 2, we provide nursing care nationwide for people with physical disabilities, and we are authorised by the Ministry of Defense to provide accompaniment and mentoring services for disabled IDF veterans, casualties of hostile action, and bereaved families. For these services, care hours are approved by the Ministry's district physician, so the process differs from a standard Bituach Leumi claim. A dedicated service is also available for veterans with a special disability rating of 100% or higher. Our page for IDF disabled veterans explains how this works.
