Home care services in Israel

Families usually reach us at a hard moment. A parent has come out of hospital, or the things they used to manage alone have quietly stopped being manageable. Our first job is to work out how much help the situation actually needs.

Caregivers assist with washing, dressing, mobility, meals, medication reminders and light housekeeping. Companionship matters too. Having someone to talk to, and someone who gets your parent out of the apartment, affects how well a person functions day to day.

Care can run from a few hours a week to a live-in arrangement, and it changes as needs change. We expect to revisit the plan rather than setting it once. More detail is on our home care and full services pages.

Elderly care, senior care and nursing care at home

Most older people would rather stay in their own home, and familiar rooms and routines genuinely help them function. The practical question is what that takes.

We begin with an assessment at home. A coordinator meets the family and the person who will receive the care, because those are two different conversations. From there we look for a caregiver who fits the household, not simply the first one available. Language, temperament, smoking, pets and religious observance are the kinds of things that decide whether a placement holds, so we would rather ask about them early than discover them later.

Our caregivers are professionally trained, and the company's professional staff stays with them throughout their employment rather than disappearing after the first week. You can read about the caregivers we place with families and the types of nursing and personal care we provide.

Round-the-clock and live-in care

Hourly help stops being enough once someone cannot safely be alone. Live-in care means a caregiver lives in the home and is there overnight. It does not mean one person on duty twenty-four hours a day, and rest time and time off need to be planned into the arrangement from the start. In practice families move to it after a fall, or when dementia reaches the point where wandering becomes a risk.

The alternative is shift cover, with several caregivers rotating so whoever is on duty is rested. It suits households with no room for someone living in. Details are on our live-in caregiver page.

Employing a foreign caregiver

Live-in care in Israel usually means employing a foreign caregiver, and that route starts with a permit. The permit is issued by the Population and Immigration Authority.

To qualify, the person needing care must be an Israeli resident above retirement age, living at home or in sheltered housing rather than an institution, and needing supervision and help with daily activities under the National Insurance dependency criteria. You submit an application with supporting documents to a Population and Immigration Authority office and pay the fee. Where someone is being discharged from hospital and the need is immediate, a temporary three-month permit can bridge the gap.

One point is worth being clear about, because it surprises families. On this route you are the employer, not us. The permit is issued in the name of the person receiving care, and the household carries the employer's obligations for wages, conditions and severance. That is different from hourly care arranged through the benefit, where the caregiver works through us. Ask us to walk you through which arrangement you are actually entering before you commit.

Once the permit is approved you register with a licensed private employment bureau from the list on the Authority's website, and that bureau handles the placement. Talk to us about taking that on for you. We look for a caregiver suited to the household rather than the first one available, and we prepare the family for what sharing a home with a caregiver actually involves. If a placement does not work out, talk to us about the options rather than assuming you are stuck with it.

Our step-by-step guides cover how to obtain a permit and what employing a foreign caregiver involves.

The long-term care benefit from Bituach Leumi

The long-term care benefit (gimlat si'ud) is paid by the National Insurance Institute, known in Israel as Bituach Leumi, and for most families it covers a real share of the cost. It is provided as weekly care hours.

Eligibility is decided by the state, not by us. After you file a claim, an assessor sent by Bituach Leumi, usually a nurse, physiotherapist or occupational therapist, carries out a dependency test covering washing, dressing, eating, mobility and cognitive function. This is usually done by telephone with the person concerned or a family member, though it can also be done as a visit to the home. The result places the claimant on one of six levels.

The hours depend on the level and on who provides the care.

  • With an Israeli caregiver, the levels run 5.5, 10, 15, 21, 26 and 30 hours a week.
  • If you employ a foreign caregiver, the allocation is lower from level three upward: 12, 17, 22 and 26 hours.

Worth checking before you choose a route. At the top level the difference is four hours a week.

Claims take time, and need does not wait. While a claim is pending we provide interim care free of charge for up to 60 days, from a minimum of six hours a week, so a family is not left managing alone through the assessment. We also help prepare the claim, and our eligibility calculator gives you a preliminary read on your chances before you file. Consultation and guidance are free.

Alzheimer's and dementia care

Dementia care asks something different of a caregiver, and not everyone is suited to it. Consistency is what helps: the same person, the same routine, the same order of things each morning. Agitation tends to worsen when the environment keeps shifting, so a stable placement matters more here than in ordinary elderly care.

We look for caregivers with experience of cognitive decline who can handle the parts families find hardest, including repeated questions, restlessness at night, and no longer being recognized by the person they look after. You can find more background on our Alzheimer's care and dementia information pages.

Care for IDF disabled veterans

Through our subsidiary Nativ 2 we provide nursing care nationwide for people with physical disabilities, and we are authorized by the Ministry of Defense to provide accompaniment and mentoring for disabled IDF veterans, casualties of hostile action, and bereaved families. Care hours on this track are approved by the Ministry's district physician, so the process differs from a standard Bituach Leumi claim. There is also a dedicated service for veterans with a special disability rating of 100% or higher. Our page for IDF disabled veterans sets out how it works.
