What Happens Next — 3 Workstreams
Now that we know what people are searching for and how your competitors rank, we use that data to take action. Every step below is based directly on the research we already completed — nothing is chosen randomly.
1

Optimization Reports for Existing Pages

Your website already has pages that appear in Google search results — but many of them are not fully optimized for the keywords people actually search for. This means small, targeted improvements to these existing pages can push them higher in the rankings and bring in more visitors, without needing to create anything new. This is usually the fastest way to see results.
  • We review each page on your site — homepage, service pages, product pages, and existing blog articles
  • For each page, we compare it against the keywords it should rank for and check: Does the page title match what people type into Google? Does the page have enough detailed content? Is it linking to and from the right related pages on your site?
  • We also check what your competitors' pages look like for the same keywords — if their page has 1,500 words of detailed content and yours has 200, that's a clear gap to fix
  • You receive one report per page with a specific list of changes — not general advice, but exact recommendations like "change the page title to X" or "add a section about Y"
Example: If your silicone extrusion page currently ranks on page 2 of Google for "medical silicone extrusion services", we analyze exactly why it's not on page 1 — maybe the title doesn't include the keyword, the content is shorter than competitors, or no other pages on your site link to it. The report tells you precisely what to fix.
What you receive: A detailed optimization report for each page, listing the current state and the recommended changes. Your development team or content team can then implement the updates.
2

New Blog Topic Proposals

During the keyword research and competitive analysis, we found search terms that your potential customers type into Google — but your website has no content about those topics yet. If someone searches for a topic and your site doesn't have a page about it, you simply won't appear in the results. New articles fill these gaps. Importantly: every topic we propose comes directly from the keyword data and competitor research. Nothing is chosen randomly or based on guesswork.
  • Each proposed topic targets a specific group of keywords with proven monthly search volume — real people searching for this right now
  • We cross-check every topic against your competitors: if 3 out of 5 top competitors have content about a topic and you don't, that's a priority gap
  • We also look for topics where competitors have weak or incomplete content — these are opportunities for you to create something better and outrank them
  • Every topic is submitted to you for review and approval before any writing begins — you decide what gets written
  • Once approved, we prepare a detailed brief for the content writer: target keywords, recommended structure (headings), key points to cover, questions to answer, and suggested word count
Example: Our research might show that 800 people per month search for "catheter prototyping process" but your site has no page about this topic. Three competitors do. We would propose an article covering catheter prototyping — targeting this keyword and related terms — and prepare a full brief showing the writer exactly what to include, based on what the top-ranking pages cover and where they fall short.
What you receive: A list of proposed topics with the search data behind each one (why this topic, how many people search for it, what competitors cover). After your approval, a full article brief per topic.
3

Support Articles for External Link Building

Google ranks websites based on two main factors: the quality of your own content (which we address in workstreams 1 and 2) and how many other trusted websites link to yours. When a reputable industry website includes a link to your page, Google sees that as a vote of confidence — it signals that your content is valuable and trustworthy. More quality links = higher rankings for competitive keywords. We create articles specifically designed to be published on relevant external websites, with links pointing back to your most important pages.
  • Each article is built around your highest-priority keywords and topics — the same ones from the keyword research
  • The articles are written to be genuinely useful and relevant to the audience of the external site — this is what makes them publishable
  • Each article contains a natural link back to a specific page on your QMD website — for example, an article about silicone biocompatibility published on a medical industry blog, linking back to your silicone materials page
  • Over time, this builds your site's authority in Google's eyes, which improves rankings across all your pages — not just the one being linked to
Example: We write an in-depth article about "advances in medical-grade silicone for catheter applications" and publish it on a relevant medical device industry website. The article includes a link to your silicone capabilities page. When Google sees that a trusted industry site is referencing your page, it boosts your page's authority and ranking potential.
What you receive: Finished articles ready for publication, with the target external sites identified. Each article includes a strategic link pointing to a specific page on your site.

Quarterly Roadmap
Each phase builds on the one before it. We start with choosing the right keywords, then optimize what you already have, then create new content to fill gaps, and finally build external credibility. Here is the sequence:
1
Phase 1

Keyword Finalization & Prioritization

We review the full list of 573 keywords together and select the ones to focus on first. Not all keywords are equal — a keyword with 5,000 monthly searches where you're already on page 2 is a much better opportunity than one with 50 searches where you have no existing page. We prioritize based on three factors: how many people search for it, how relevant it is to your business, and how realistic it is for you to rank for it.

Why this matters: This keyword list drives every decision that follows — which pages we optimize, which articles we write, which topics we target. If we optimize for the wrong keywords, the traffic we bring won't convert into leads. Getting this right is the foundation of the entire strategy.
In Progress
2
Phase 2

Existing Page Optimization Reports

Once the keywords are confirmed, we go through each existing page on your site and compare it to the keywords it should rank for. We produce a detailed report per page showing exactly what needs to change. This includes: rewriting the page title to include the target keyword, improving the meta description (the short text that appears in Google results), restructuring the content with proper headings, expanding thin content where competitors have more detail, and adding internal links between related pages on your site.

Why this matters: Your site already has pages that Google knows about and indexes. Making targeted improvements to these pages is the fastest way to see ranking improvements — because you're building on something that already exists, rather than waiting for Google to discover entirely new pages.

3
Phase 3

New Blog Topic Planning & Approval

Based on the keyword gaps we identified, we prepare a list of new article topics. Each topic is backed by data: the specific keywords it targets, the monthly search volume, and the competitor landscape (how many competitors cover this topic and how well). We submit this list for your approval — you choose which topics to move forward with. Once approved, we create a full brief for each article that tells the writer exactly what to cover, what structure to use, and what questions to answer.

Why this matters: Right now, when someone searches for a topic related to your services that you don't have content about, your site simply doesn't appear. Every new article you publish is a new opportunity for Google to show your site in search results. Over time, this dramatically increases the number of search queries where your site is visible.

4
Phase 4

Support Content for Authority & Link Building

We create high-quality articles designed for publication on external industry websites and blogs — not on your own site. Each article naturally includes a link back to one of your key pages. For example, an article about regulatory trends in medical device manufacturing, published on a relevant industry publication, with a link to your regulatory services page. These are not generic articles — each one is built around your priority keywords and connects back to a strategic page on your site.

Why this matters: Google's ranking algorithm heavily weighs how many other websites link to yours. A link from a trusted industry website tells Google: "this company is a legitimate, authoritative source." This authority builds up over time and improves your rankings across the board — not just for the specific page being linked to, but for your entire domain.
