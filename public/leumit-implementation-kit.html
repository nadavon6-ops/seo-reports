'''Leumit Health Services''' ({{lang-he|לאומית שירותי בריאות}}, formerly '''Kupat Holim Leumit''') is the smallest of Israel's four [[health maintenance organization|health funds]] (''kupot holim''), providing healthcare services to approximately 773,000 members (~9% of the insured population).<ref name="NII2024">National Insurance Institute of Israel, Annual Report 2024.</ref> Founded in November 1933 by a group of physicians affiliated with the [[Revisionist Zionism|Revisionist Zionist movement]], the organization was linked to the [[National Workers' Union (Israel)|National Workers' Union]] until its gradual separation beginning with the [[National Health Insurance Law (Israel)|National Health Insurance Law]] of 1995 and completed in 2016.<ref name="NHIL">National Health Insurance Law, 1994; IJHPR, 2020.</ref>

Leumit operates a nationwide network of approximately 320–350 clinics, ~150 pharmacies (the "Leumit Pharm" chain, third-largest in Israel), ~70 optical centers, and a central laboratory processing over one million tests per month.<ref name="Veeam">Veeam Customer Story: Leumit Health Services.</ref>

According to the Ministry of Health's 2024 comparative report, Leumit recorded the lowest complaint rate among all four health funds (2.78 per 10,000 members) and ranked first in appointment accessibility.<ref name="MOH2024">Ministry of Health, Comparative Report 2024.</ref>

== History ==

=== Founding during the Mandate period (1933–1948) ===

Leumit was established in November 1933 by a group of physicians led by Dr. Yosef Paemoni and Dr. Yaakov Weinshal. Other founders included Dr. Nahum Kahanovitz, Dr. Emil Rosenberg, Dr. Alexander Davidson, Dr. Moshe Har-Even, Dr. Mordechai Giladi, and Dr. Zvi Hirsh Glicker.<ref name="Hamichlol">Hamichlol – Leumit Health Services.</ref>

The fund was created in the wake of dismissals or resignations of Revisionist physicians from [[Clalit Health Services|Kupat Holim Clalit]] following accusations related to the [[Assassination of Haim Arlosoroff|assassination of Haim Arlosoroff]] in June 1933. The first clinic operated from Dr. Weinshal's private residence on Gruzenberg Street in [[Tel Aviv]]. In January 1934, the fund received formal recognition from [[Mandatory Palestine|British Mandate]] authorities, and later that year affiliated with the newly established [[National Workers' Union (Israel)|National Workers' Union]].<ref name="Hamichlol"/>

Its first director, David Malamudovitch, established the principle that the fund would be "open to all without political or class restrictions."<ref name="Hamichlol"/> By 1935, branches had opened in [[Jerusalem]] and [[Haifa]], and in 1937 in [[Ramat Gan]]. On May 18, 1944, the fund was officially registered as the "Association for the Health Fund of National Workers."<ref name="Hamichlol"/>

=== Statehood era (1948–1995) ===

Following the [[Israeli Declaration of Independence|establishment of Israel]] in 1948, Leumit operated in [[Ma'abarot|transit camps]], immigrant settlements, and [[Development town|development towns]], contributing healthcare services during mass immigration. In the early 1960s, the fund transitioned to a mixed model of independent and salaried physicians.<ref name="Hamichlol"/>

After the [[Six-Day War]] (1967), Leumit became the primary healthcare organization serving settlements in the [[occupied territories]].<ref name="Hamichlol"/>

=== National Health Insurance Law and independence (1995–2016) ===

In June 1994, the [[Knesset]] passed the [[National Health Insurance Law (Israel)|National Health Insurance Law]], which took effect in January 1995. The law mandated universal membership in one of four health funds, severed organizational ties between health funds and labor unions, and established a uniform healthcare basket funded through the [[National Insurance Institute of Israel|National Insurance Institute]].<ref name="NHIL"/>

Leumit's full organizational separation from the National Workers' Union was completed in 2016, when a new charter eliminated the union's authority to appoint directors.<ref name="Hamichlol"/>

=== Rebranding ===

On May 2, 2010, the fund changed its name from "Kupat Holim Leumit" to "Leumit Health Services" and updated its logo.<ref name="Hamichlol"/> In January 2026, Leumit launched a comprehensive visual rebrand, transitioning from its traditional blue-green color scheme to purple, with a new logo, font, clinic designs, and digital assets.<ref name="JPost2026">Jerusalem Post, January 2026.</ref>

== Infrastructure and services ==

=== Clinics and laboratories ===

Leumit operates approximately 320–350 clinics and branches nationwide. Its central laboratory processes over one million tests per month.<ref name="Veeam"/>

=== Service networks ===

* '''"Leumit Pharm"''' – a chain of ~150 pharmacies, the third-largest pharmacy network in Israel<ref name="Hamichlol"/>
* '''"Leumit Dent"''' – dental clinics (expanded since 2026)<ref name="JPost2026"/>
* '''Optical centers''' – ~70 locations<ref name="NBN">Nefesh B'Nefesh – Leumit Health Services.</ref>
* '''Complementary medicine''' – [[acupuncture]], [[chiropractic]], [[homeopathy]], [[reflexology]], and [[tai chi]]<ref name="Hamichlol"/>

=== Hospital partnerships ===

In 2024, Leumit acquired a stake in Medica, a private hospital network that purchased Raphael Hospital in Tel Aviv for NIS 550 million. Medica also includes Elisha Hospital in Haifa, Rosen Center in Afula, and the NARA clinic network.<ref name="JPost2024">Jerusalem Post, 2024.</ref> Leumit also participates in planning the Shimon Peres Hospital in [[Be'er Sheva]], a joint initiative with [[Meuhedet]] to be operated by [[Sheba Medical Center]].<ref name="JPost2024"/>

== Innovation and technology ==

=== Electronic medical records ===

Leumit operates an EMR system called "Tamar," built on a single central database from inception, containing over 25 years of longitudinal medical data including diagnoses, prescriptions, lab results, referrals, and procedures.<ref name="ResearchInstitute">Leumit Research Institute, leumit.co.il.</ref>

=== FHIR implementation ===

Leumit is implementing the [[FHIR]] (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) standard using InterSystems IRIS for Health. A pilot project with [[Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center|Sourasky Medical Center]] (Ichilov) deployed an automated eligibility determination system via hospital admission kiosks. Leumit aims to be the first organization in Israel to report to the Ministry of Health using HL7 FHIR.<ref name="InterSystems">InterSystems Success Story: Leumit.</ref>

=== Leumit Start ===

Established in 2018, "Leumit Start" is the fund's digital health and innovation arm, offering startups clinical expert access, anonymized clinical data via the [[MDclone]] platform, regulatory guidance including Helsinki Committee (IRB) support, and design partnership programs. In May 2022, Leumit Start launched the "Leumit WeCcelerate" accelerator for digital health innovation.<ref name="CTech">CTech/Calcalist, May 2022.</ref>

== Research Institute ==

Leumit's Research Institute was established in 2019 and is directed by Dr. Ariel Israel (Yehuda), MD, PhD, of [[Tel Aviv University]], who has published over 100 scientific articles.<ref name="TAU">Dr. Ariel Israel, Tel Aviv University.</ref> Research areas include epidemiology, telemedicine, AI/big data, clinical pharmacology, and personalized medicine.<ref name="ResearchInstitute"/>

Notable publications based on Leumit's database include studies on diabetes care quality (MESSAGE program, ''ScienceDirect'', 2021), G6PD deficiency and autoimmune risk (''PMC'', 2023), and NLP analysis of Hebrew electronic medical records (''PubMed'', 2024).<ref name="ScienceDirect">ScienceDirect, 2021.</ref><ref name="PMC">PMC, 2023.</ref><ref name="PubMed">PubMed, 2024.</ref>

== Recognition ==

* '''Lowest complaint rate''': 2.78 per 10,000 members (2024), lowest among all four health funds for two consecutive years<ref name="MOH2024"/>
* '''#1 appointment accessibility''': shortest wait times for gynecology, dermatology, and orthopedics (2024)<ref name="MOH2024"/>
* '''Best workplace in healthcare''': ranked #1 by BDI/TheMarker for nine consecutive years (2017–2025), #46 overall<ref name="BDI">Leumit BDI Best Workplace 2025.</ref>

== Challenges ==

Leumit is the smallest of Israel's four health funds with ~9% market share, compared to Clalit (~52%), Maccabi (~25%), and Meuhedet (~14%).<ref name="NII2024"/> According to the [[Taub Center for Social Policy Studies in Israel|Taub Center]], the fund has consistently lost more members than it gained since 2006.<ref name="Taub">Taub Center, December 2023.</ref>
